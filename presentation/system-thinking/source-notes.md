# Systems thinking — source notes

**Source:** *"How To Think SO Clearly People Assume You're Brilliant"* — Sandeep Swadia (theMITmonk), YouTube, ~20 min.
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mjTgkm-h__M>

These are structured notes in our own words (not a transcript) — watch the video for the full story.

## 1. What a system is
- A **system** is a set of connected parts that keeps producing a pattern. Your company, your career and your relationships are all systems.
- **Systems thinking** = seeing the hidden pattern behind the parts you can observe.
- Method: **parts → connections → patterns.** Example: a coffee shop — customer, menu, cashier, barista, machine, all linked by the order flow.
- Opening image: firefighters read the colour of smoke as information about what is burning — the visible signal tells you about the hidden system.
- Practice: in any shop or office, ask *what are the hidden parts, how are they connected, which patterns repeat?*

## 2. Three reasons people misread systems
1. **Wrong system type** → wrong approach (see §3).
2. **Bad incentives — the Cobra Effect.** A bounty on dead cobras led people to breed cobras. Reward the wrong thing and the system optimises for the reward, not the goal.
3. **Delayed feedback.** When the pleasure is immediate and the damage arrives years later (smoking), the loop is invisible.

## 3. Four types of systems
| Type | Cause → effect | How to act | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clear** | Obvious | Follow the process, use checklists | Recipes, pilots, surgeons; Van Halen's "no brown M&Ms" clause as a signal the contract was read |
| **Complicated** | Hidden but discoverable | Slow down, analyse, find the right expert | Emergency diagnosis, tax, M&A due diligence |
| **Complex** | Only visible in hindsight | Small experiments, adapt, be directionally right | Merging cultures after an acquisition; getting people to adopt AI |
| **Chaotic** | Broken, constantly shifting | Act first, stabilise, then understand | The 1982 Tylenol recall; natural disasters |

Key line for AI: *building an AI roadmap is complicated; getting people to change how they work with AI is complex.*

## 4. DART — finding out which system you're in
- **D**econstruct — break it into parts; are they stable or shifting?
- **A**nalyse — how do cause and effect relate? (the most important step)
- **R**ecognise — have I seen this pattern before, here or elsewhere?
- **T**est — the smallest experiment before committing (skip in chaos).

## 5. The hidden loop: systems train you
- Every system you live in is quietly training you.
- **Train metaphor:** from inside a train you can't tell which train is moving — you need an outside view.
- Three outside views: **mentors** (no stake in your story), **data** (doesn't care about your narrative), **time** (compare yourself to a year ago).

## 6. False either/or choices
Many "luxury *or* scale" style trade-offs are limits of the current system design, not of reality — redesign the system and both become possible.
