Definition

A system is a set of connected parts that keeps producing a pattern

Systems thinking is seeing the hidden pattern behind the parts you can observe. Firefighters read the colour of smoke to learn what's burning behind the door.

Your company is a system

Parts, connections, and the one pattern that pays the bills

PartsPeople, customers, products, tools, documents, know-how in heads
ConnectionsProcesses, handovers, emails, meetings, APIs between tools
PatternHow money flows: who buys, why, how often, and what it costs to deliver

Most of these parts are invisible: they live in inboxes, spreadsheets and people's heads.

The money loop

How companies make money is a feedback loop

Marketing, sales, delivery and customer success are all parts of one loop. Optimise one part in isolation and the loop can get worse.

The digital twin

A living model of your company that you can read, query and act on

The AI trap

Building the AI roadmap is complicated.
Getting people to work differently is complex.

You can hire experts for the first. The second only moves through small experiments: one skill, one team, one workflow at a time.

That's why we started today with your repeated workflow, not a company-wide plan.

Watch out · 1

The Cobra Effect: systems optimise for the reward, not the goal

Pay a bounty for dead cobras and people start breeding cobras.

Agents are no different. Measure an AI on emails sent and you get spam. Measure it on meetings booked with good-fit buyers and you get pipeline.

Before you automate a metric, ask what behaviour it rewards.

Watch out · 2

Delayed feedback hides the loop

When the payoff is instant and the cost arrives months later, nobody connects the two.

In companies

Discounts close deals today and cause churn next year. Hiring fixes capacity now and dilutes the culture later.

In your twin

Store events with timestamps in the database, so effects can be traced back to their causes.

In your skills

Add evals and logs. A skill nobody measures quietly drifts.
